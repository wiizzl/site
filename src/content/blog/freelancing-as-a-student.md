I've been freelancing since 2024, building software for small businesses and individuals, while studying computer science. Working directly with clients taught me things no course did. Here are the main ones.

## Clients buy outcomes, not technologies

Nobody has ever asked me which framework I use. They want more bookings, less paperwork, or a website they can update themselves. I learned to explain my work in terms of what it changes for them, and to keep the technical choices for myself.

## A clear scope protects everyone

The most stressful projects were the ones where the scope was vague. Now I write down what's included, what isn't, and what happens when something new comes up, before starting.

## Simple beats clever

A small business can't afford a stack that only I understand. I pick boring, well-documented tools and favour solutions they can maintain or hand over to someone else.

## Communication is half the job

A short update every week avoids most misunderstandings. Clients don't need every detail, they need to know where things stand and what's next.
