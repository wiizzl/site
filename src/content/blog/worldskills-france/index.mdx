In October 2025, I won the silver medal in Web Technologies at the 48th national competition of WorldSkills France, in Marseille, representing Normandy. Here's what the road to Marseille looked like, what I took away from it, and why I didn't just turn the page afterwards.

## What is WorldSkills?

WorldSkills is a competition of trades for young people under 23. In France, WorldSkills France runs it in numbered cycles. Each cycle starts with regional selections, then the best competitors of each region meet at the national competition. Medallists can then try to join the French team for the European (EuroSkills) and world (WorldSkills) competitions. I took part in the 48th cycle.

## Web Technologies, skill 17

Web Technologies is the web development trade. Over several days, you go through timed modules: building a frontend from a design, a backend and an API, a fullstack app, plus speed modules.

## From the regional selection to Team Normandy

I heard about WorldSkills in my first year of computer science studies, right after high school. The teacher in charge of the program noticed me, and she convinced me to sign up.

The regional selection took place in Caen, at the Lycée Jean Rostand. We were about ten candidates, and I arrived without really knowing what to expect. The test lasted a single day: six hours, on a fairly simple subject meant to check the basics of web development. One detail matters a lot in Web Technologies, at the regional selection as at the national competition: you have no internet access.

When I left the room, I was almost sure I would be selected, because I had completed everything the subject asked for. Then I received an email inviting me to CESI in Rouen for the medal ceremony. I was really happy: it meant going to Marseille, and travelling for my skills was completely new to me.

Being selected doesn't just mean you'll compete. It means you join a team: every competitor of the region, all trades together, who prepare together and travel to the national competition as one delegation.

That ceremony was the first time I met all the winners, one for each trade. I wasn't very comfortable at first. I didn't know yet that the training and the whole WorldSkills adventure would bring us so close.

## Preparing the mind and the body

The region didn't only train us on our trade. Three days of competition under a timer is also a matter of stamina and nerves, and the team prepared us for that too.

The region organized several meetups with the whole regional team, in Rouen, Caen, Houlgate and other places. Some of them lasted a whole weekend, and spending an entire weekend together brings a team closer. The goal was to build a team spirit, even if for me the competition itself would be individual, and to make sure we would arrive balanced, calm and confident.

We had team-building and sport workshops, and workshops where we had to communicate with each other. People from the Agence de l'Orientation et des Métiers de Normandie were there for the mental side and were always available to talk, about anything and everything. For the physical side, we had specialists too, like a yoga teacher.

I think this matters as much as the technical side. It teaches you to handle your stress, so you arrive confident and only have to focus on the technical work, nothing else.

## Preparing the trade

The technical preparation was the part I knew best. I trained on short exercises with a target time [[1](https://github.com/wiizzl/skill17-training), [2](https://codepen.io/collection/zzZGmz)], redid past national subjects under real time constraints, and prepared a starter project for each kind of module, so I would never lose time on setup.

My object-oriented programming teacher coached me on the technical side from start to finish. He isn't a web specialist, but he had the technical background and the big picture to support me and understand what the competition expected.

Some weeks, I couldn't train at all. But I tried to be regular and to put in several hours every week. That same teacher even gave me some of his class time to train, in conditions close to the real day: with noise and people around.

### Competing

A day of competition started very early. In the morning, we did a physical warm-up with the Normandy team to wake our muscles up. Then I arrived at my workstation, and took a moment to breathe, to get ready for the module of the day.

Our subject was inspired by the video game Outer Wilds. We had to recreate some of its mechanics, in 2D of course: navigating between planets, an inventory system, a dialogue system with several choices that depend on the items you own, and many more mechanics like these.

## After the competition: the other side

Finishing second also qualified me for the selections that decide who will be a candidate for the French team at EuroSkills Düsseldorf, in September 2027.

The competition gave me a lot, and I then learned that I could give some of it back. The position of deputy national expert in Web Technologies, a volunteer role, opened up, and I now assist the national expert, Gilles Granger, for the next cycles. He had already run the 48th cycle when I was a competitor.

As his deputy, I help him write the rules and the subjects, and organize the tests. The next national competition, the 49th, will take place in Orléans in October 2027.

From this side, it's interesting to see all the organization behind the competition. We meet on video calls, and we've been preparing since March 2026, about a year and a half before the competition.
